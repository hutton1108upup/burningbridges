Burning Bridges Audio and Posting Guide

Inspect audio, choose permitted sounds in TikTok or Reels, avoid doubled tracks and review landscape-to-vertical composition.

Updated October 13, 2026

On this page
  • Check what is actually in the file
  • Post with TikTok’s available sound options
  • Prepare an Instagram Reel
  • Check the crop before posting
  • Check current platform guidance

Check what is actually in the file

Play the entire video with sound. A prompt naming a song is not an audio file, and a reference clip containing music does not give you publishing rights. The current site shows existing reference media; it does not promise a new performance with the original soundtrack.

  • If the file is silent, choose permitted audio separately.
  • If it already has audio, check that you may use it and avoid layering two competing tracks.
  • Compare gesture timing and sound before export; a finished file can still be out of sync.

Post with TikTok’s available sound options

  1. Save the video you are entitled to publish to your phone.
  2. Open TikTok, start a new post and select the clip.
  3. Open the sound picker and look for a track available for your account and region. Check the displayed artist and permitted use.
  4. Preview the selected segment, adjust its start against a recognizable gesture, and reduce the original track if it doubles the new one.
  5. Watch the post preview with sound and check captions and the AI disclosure options before publishing.

Labels and available sounds differ by app version, region and account type. Do not assume finding a track authorizes an advertisement or every kind of reuse.

Prepare an Instagram Reel

  1. Start a Reel and select the permitted video.
  2. Open the music or audio picker and choose an available permitted track.
  3. Adjust the segment and original audio level; listen for overlapping tracks.
  4. Preview the cover, crop, caption position and disclosure options before sharing.

If the intended track is absent, choose another sound you may use or publish without it. Do not upload an unlicensed recording just to work around availability.

Check the crop before posting

9:16 is a useful vertical composition for short-video feeds; 16:9 keeps a wider room and crowd. Our current references are landscape. Cropping a wide video may cut off the cup, hands or guests. Choosing 9:16 in the form records an intended setting, not a newly composed version of the reference file.

Keep text away from areas covered by app controls, then review on a phone. More pixels do not fix an awkward crop. Inspect the references and check audio problems.

Check current platform guidance

Reviewed October 13, 2026. Music availability and posting controls can change. Refer to TikTok’s video-creation help and Instagram Help Center for your current app and account.
