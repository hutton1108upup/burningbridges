How to Make The Jawn AI Dance Video from a Photo

Make a Jawn AI dance video by starting with one clear photo and studying the timing: a quiet wait, an expression change and a bounce on the beat. This guide walks through choosing a person or pet, preparing the image and checking the resulting clip.

By Burning Bridges AI Video · Updated November 2, 2026

  • Frequently asked questions

1. Choose a person or pet photo

Choose one subject with a clear face and even light. For a person, leave some room around the head and shoulders; for a cat or dog, keep the eyes, ears and distinctive markings visible. Use an image you own or have permission to animate. A blurry group shot makes it harder to tell which face should lead the scene.

2. Study the wait before the bounce

Play the example once without concentrating on the soundtrack. Look for the still expression, the small gesture and the moment the subject begins to move. That pause is what makes the change in energy readable. If a cup appears, check whether the hand and cup remain separate as the body moves.

3. Select your photo in the maker

Open the Jawn dance maker, press Generate video and choose your image in the photo workspace. The selector accepts JPG, PNG and WebP files up to 10 MB. You can replace the selected photo or remove it and start again. Selecting a file shows a local preview; it does not itself submit a generation task.

4. Check the plan and start your workflow

After a valid image is selected, use Generate video in the workspace to open Pricing. Review the displayed plans and current requirements before continuing. Keep your original image available: the portrait you preview and the final video are separate files.

5. Review identity, hands and movement

Watch the entire clip at normal speed, then pause at the expression change and the first dance movement. Check whether the person or pet remains recognizable; inspect ears, hair, hands and any drink. If a detail changes or disappears, simplify the input and compare another attempt only after reviewing the current cost.

6. Add audio and prepare the final post

The trend is associated with The JAWN by Jerri, but do not assume a particular soundtrack is included in your result. Check the actual file and the audio terms of the platform or editor you use. If you add a permitted track, align the first bounce with the beat, avoid doubled sound and test the exported file on a phone.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos do I need?

The photo workspace asks for one image of a person or pet.

Can I use a pet picture?

A clear image of one cat or dog is a useful starting point. Keep the face and distinctive markings visible.

Does selecting a photo create the video?

Selecting a photo shows a local preview. Use Generate video after selecting a valid photo to continue to pricing.

Will a prompt reproduce the exact routine?

A written description can communicate a timing idea, but it does not guarantee the same gestures or choreography. Review the actual result.
