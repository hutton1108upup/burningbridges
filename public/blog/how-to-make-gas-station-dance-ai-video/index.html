How to Make a Gas Station Dance AI Video

To make a Gas Station Dance AI video, assign a person to each of three shots: the opening dance, the selfie and the car exit. Prepare the photos for those roles, choose a template that supports your cast, and check every cut in the result. Solo and trio versions need different input planning.

By Burning Bridges AI Video · October 20, 2026

What you can do here: Gas Station Dance AI provides reference material and preparation guidance. It does not generate this effect. The rendering steps below require a compatible external tool. Burning Bridges plans cover the separate party template.

Map the three shots before choosing a tool

Write down the cast in shot order. A trio means three people appearing in successive shots; it does not necessarily mean three dancers standing together. If you want the same person throughout, select a supported solo mode or follow the provider's instructions for reusing an image across roles.

Watch our reference and decide where each face should appear. The opening needs enough room for the dance, the selfie brings the face close to the camera, and the final shot must show the person leaving the car. A template with a similar title may use a different cut or number of people.

Choose a different crop for each role

For the dancer, choose a full-body photo with clear hands and shoes. For the selfie, prioritize a sharp front-facing portrait. For the car exit, include the body and feet so clothing and stance are visible. Keep each file to one person; a group photo does not tell a generator which face belongs in which shot.

Our planning page has Just me and Me + 2 friends controls. It accepts JPG, PNG or WebP up to 8 MB each, at least 512 × 512 pixels. These limits apply to the local preview only. Switching modes helps plan your cast; it does not create a video or carry the images into checkout.

For more framing checks, see the photo preparation guide; use this project’s stated file limits where they differ.

Check the template's role order and audio

Gasstationdance.co describes separate dancer, selfie and car-exit inputs plus an option to use one person. That makes its documented role map useful when comparing a provider with your storyboard. Check the current controls yourself; another service may require three files even for the same person.

If you build the sequence in a general video editor, treat each shot as a separate task and assemble them in order. This gives you control over cuts, but also makes you responsible for keeping the person, outfit and lighting consistent. A single broad prompt is a poor place to manage three independent identities.

Make and review your first video

  1. Choose solo or trio

    Use the local project page to organize your photos. In an external generator, find the equivalent cast mode and verify its labels. Do not infer that a mode exists merely because the reference contains several people.

  2. Upload in shot order

    Assign the dancer first, selfie performer second and car-exit person third where those are the documented roles. Recheck every preview before continuing, especially when filenames look alike.

  3. Confirm the complete sequence

    Watch the provider's sample through the end. Read the duration, framing, audio and cost shown for your selection. A short dance-only effect will not become a three-shot story just by adding more portraits.

  4. Inspect the cuts in the draft

    Pause just before and after the selfie transition and the car-door reveal. Check that the intended person appears in each shot and that a hand, door or phone does not merge with the face.

  5. Finish one sound track and export

    Listen to the downloaded clip first. Keep or replace its audio deliberately rather than stacking two music tracks. Reopen the exported file and confirm that the final car-exit shot was not trimmed away.

Fix the most visible problem first

The same person appears in every shot
Check whether you selected solo mode or reused the same file in all roles. More detailed prompt wording cannot substitute for a missing cast input.
The outfit changes between shots
For a solo version, choose photos with consistent clothing or use one source if supported. If you are making separate clips, keep the appearance description stable across all three.
The car door clips through the performer
Inspect the body and door movement together. Retry the specific shot when the workflow permits it rather than changing all three cast photos.

Save the first draft and change one input at a time. The video troubleshooting guide explains how to separate input problems from a completed result that needs revision.

Frequently asked questions

Can I make the gas station dance with one photo?

Some providers offer a solo mode. Our Just me control previews one person for the sequence; check the external generator's required inputs separately.

Are three photos three simultaneous dancers?

In the sequence covered here, they map to the opening dancer, selfie performer and car-exit person in successive shots.

Does buying Burning Bridges credits create this dance?

No. This project page is for references and local photo planning. Current Burning Bridges plans cover the party template, not the gas station sequence.

Put the plan into practice

Start with the Gas Station Dance AI reference, choose the inputs described above, and check one complete draft before making more versions.
