Whose song and performance are these?

Alors on danse is associated with Belgian musician Stromae. The white-studio reference features Stromae and French comedian Jamel Debbouze. It is a staged comedy scene, not documentary proof that Jamel wrote the hit.

The Jamel TV upload identifies it as part of Made in Jamel (2010). Le Progrès reported on December 4, 2010 that Jamel posted the sketch to promote his DVD, pretending to instruct Stromae. Sources: Jamel TV, “Alors on danse, le tube”; Le Progrès, “Un son de canard malade…”.

What is the joke in the studio?

The scene presents an absurd creative partnership: one person plays and assembles music while the other supplies instructions and reactions. Serious-looking music work is interrupted by comic advice.

The white room, laptop and small keyboard make the two roles easy to recognize. Replacing the performers changes the joke while preserving the interaction. Describing it only as two people dancing misses the setup.

What is documented about its circulation?

Le Progrès covered the sketch’s online success in December 2010. That places its circulation long before modern AI duets. A later upload date is another distribution point, not evidence that the scene was newly filmed.

The reference collection and AI-style recasts are a further use of that interaction. Interpretation: assigning other people or characters to the two roles gives an existing scene a new comic contrast. The first AI swap and its precise breakout date are not established by this review.

Song, music video and sketch are different

The song is the musical work. Its conventional music video and the comic “making-of” are different presentations. An AI duet adds another layer of adaptation.

Calling the sketch a real songwriting documentary misrepresents its premise. Calling an AI recast an official collaboration misrepresents the participants. Our examples are independent references, not endorsements by either performer.
