Four steps from photo to finished clip

  1. Choose a movement reference

    Open the Firestarter examples. Watch the complete clip and choose the gestures and framing you want to work toward.

  2. Prepare one clear photo

    Use the photo area to check a JPG, PNG or WebP up to 10 MB. Keep the face clear and leave room for both arms. See the photo checklist.

  3. Review the creation details

    Check the template or movement input, length, frame shape, resolution and credit total before confirming. Use Pricing to review current plans; use the workflow comparison to understand the inputs.

  4. Check and save the result

    Watch the full clip, pause on the largest gesture and compare it with your photo. Check face, hands and feet, then check the saved file and audio.

How can I turn a selfie into a Firestarter dance video?

Start with a clear portrait and match it to your chosen workflow. A tightly cropped face contains little information about the arms, outfit or legs, so it may not be a suitable starting frame for a full-body animation.

  1. Prepare the image. Prefer one visible face, even lighting and enough room for the planned movement. A wider portrait is often easier to work with than a close-up selfie.

  2. Choose the movement. Use a matching preset, or supply a permitted reference if the tool supports it. Confirm the inputs rather than assuming every upload box accepts a video.

  3. Test and inspect. Start with a short draft. Check the face, hands, feet and crop before spending more on an extended result.

What if I only have a close-up selfie?

You can take a wider photo, use a workflow that explicitly accepts a face photo, or prepare a wider image in an editor. Outpainting invents missing visual information; it cannot recover your real unseen clothes or body. Review the edited image before animating it.

Check the crop before you start: leave space above the head and beside both arms.

Can AI copy the same dance and keep my face, outfit and background?

A motion reference can guide movement more directly than text alone, but it does not guarantee identical choreography or perfect preservation of appearance. Face identity, clothing, body shape and background stability are separate things to inspect.

Compare frames before, during and after a turn. Look for face changes, merged hands, moving clothing details and a background that bends with the dancer. If your outfit or room is essential, confirm that the chosen workflow uses the starting image rather than replacing only a face in other footage.

How should I frame the dancer and neon scene?

The reference site presents Firestarter as a K/DA-inspired dance concept. Its samples use strong colored light and bright contours around a single performer. Treat that as a visual reference, not a guarantee about a model or an official affiliation.

Build a starting image where the person is readable without particles. Place the subject away from the edges and keep a clear floor if the feet are visible. Use a darker background behind pale clothing, or lighter accents around a dark outfit, while leaving the face recognizable.

Start with a fixed camera. Combining a rotating camera, a body turn and moving lights in the first draft makes it difficult to isolate the cause of a distorted frame.

A dark stage with restrained purple background light and a warm rim light around the subject. Keep the face naturally readable, the hands separate from the torso and the visible feet grounded. No cuts, extra people or sudden wardrobe changes.

For a simpler test: remove the arm accent and keep only the weight shift. For a character: explicitly ask to preserve the existing illustration style and proportions. Add a small direction change only after the first motion stays stable.

Keep each revision focused. If the hands deform, simplify the gesture before adding more lighting instructions. If the face changes, reduce the turn before changing the whole scene.

How do I check the first dance draft?

Watch once at normal speed, then pause during the largest gesture and any turn. Compare the face and outfit with the source image. A dramatic glow can hide errors that become obvious in a paused frame.

Can I make a Firestarter AI dance video on my phone?

You can prepare a photo and review references in a phone browser. Actual generation and saving depend on the tool you choose; check its current mobile support before buying.

  1. Select a permitted photo from your device and inspect its crop.
  2. Open the supported template or reference workflow and check the settings.
  3. Keep the task link or account history so you can return to the result.
  4. Use the tool’s download action and locate the file in your browser downloads or Files app. Saving to Photos may require a separate share action.

Do not assume every web tool can save directly to the camera roll or share automatically to TikTok. For trial costs and export restrictions, see what to check before using an AI trial.

What should I check about music and the exported file?

Do not assume a reference soundtrack is included with a generation plan. Use audio you have permission to publish, and check the options available for your account on the destination platform. Inspect the motion without sound first, then adjust timing against your chosen track.

Open the exported file and check the first and last frames, face detail, visible hands and feet, and whether the final crop matches your intended post. Apply the platform’s current AI-content labeling options where required. A successful preview is not proof that the downloaded file has the same framing or audio.

Firestarter AI dance video FAQ

What is the easiest way to make a Firestarter dance video?

A ready-made template can reduce setup if its full sample matches your goal. If the movement must match a particular clip, check for motion-reference support. Neither route guarantees a fixed completion time.

Can I use an ordinary selfie?

A clear selfie can provide face detail. For a dance that shows arms, clothing or legs, use a wider portrait and check the photo requirements for the selected workflow.

Can a prompt reproduce the exact dance?

A prompt describes actions but does not guarantee exact choreography. Use a compatible motion-reference workflow when precise movement is important, and still inspect the result.

Will it preserve my clothes and background perfectly?

Compare the generated face, outfit and background with your original photo. Pay particular attention to turns, overlapping hands and changes in lighting.

Does a phone preview mean a downloadable MP4 is included?

No. Browser playback, generation and downloading are separate capabilities. Check export conditions before paying.

How can I make the Firestarter dance look smoother?

Start with a clear single-person reference and keep the camera movement simple. Review the hands, face and feet through the whole clip, then adjust one setting at a time so you can compare results.

Choose a portrait on the Firestarter page, or compare the references again. Keep one clear goal for your first version.
