How to Make a Smalltown Boy AI Video

Build a Smalltown Boy two-character video plan: assign the portraits, map the changing positions and reaction, and review face consistency and spacing.

1. Choose the result and workflow

Start here: Use a workflow that can preserve two identities within the same scene. Build a clear starting pair and animate a small exchange first. If the effect depends on an exact sung phrase or high note, treat audio alignment as its own editing task rather than assuming the motion model understands the music.

If you are still choosing the look, read what the Smalltown Boy AI trend is. This guide describes a planning and external-creation workflow. The local Smalltown Boy page supplies references and photo preparation, not this scene’s generation.

2. Prepare the photo inputs

Use one clear image for each character, with eyes, hairline and shoulders visible. Aim for comparable sharpness. If one image is a drawing and the other a photograph, decide whether the mixed style is intentional before building the scene.

Open the local photo preparation area to inspect the selected files. Follow the format and size rules printed beside its inputs. Then check the requirements of your chosen creation tool separately; they may differ.

3. Plan a short sequence

Introduce the pairing

Give both characters visible space. Let the viewer identify each person before the positions or emphasis change.

Pass the attention

Plan a glance, turn or small change of position that moves attention from one partner to the other.

Land the reaction

Hold the final expression long enough to read it. Avoid ending immediately as the face begins to react.

Use these as separate composition checks. A service with multi-shot controls may accept them together; otherwise, test a short shot first and join usable clips in an editor. Do not assume a single upload supports every camera change.

4. Adapt an original starting prompt

For a compatible image-to-video tool, this is a starting idea rather than a tested preset or a guarantee of the reference choreography. Keep only the actions your selected model can handle.

Two distinct adult characters share a simple performance space. One turns slightly toward the partner, the second responds with an expressive smile, then both settle into a final pose. Preserve each face and outfit, keep their identities separate as attention shifts, medium-wide steady camera, no new characters or text.

Choose the appearance references through the tool’s supported input controls. A written instruction to preserve a face cannot replace a missing identity input. Adjust the framing to your intended output before spending credits on longer clips.

5. Review the first draft

The two faces become similar
Use more distinct input portraits and clearer spacing. Compare each person against their own input, not only against the other character.
The reaction happens too early
Trim the lead-in or hold the first shot longer in the edit. Changing timing is often easier than regenerating the entire pair.
Position changes confuse the viewer
Start with a smaller turn instead of a full crossing. Preserve a clear screen side for each character until the pairing is established.

6. Edit, check sound and export

Trim the start and ending so the sequence has a clear setup and a settled finish. Avoid using an elaborate transition to hide a face or hand error: compare a simpler shot first. If several clips are joined, check screen direction and identity on both sides of every cut.

Use audio you are entitled to use and inspect the actual export. Check whether the tool includes audio, allows downloads and adds a watermark before rendering. The Smalltown Boy free-method guide gives an editing alternative when you prefer to avoid paid AI tests.

Choose filming when expression and timing are the priority. Choose a two-identity AI workflow when the synthetic pairing itself is the point. Start with a short reaction test rather than a long, complicated position swap.
