How to Make a Smalltown Boy AI Dance Video

To make a Smalltown Boy AI dance video, choose two clear character photos and decide who takes the first close-up. Use a duo template that matches the swap-and-dance reference, then inspect both identities as they change positions. Check the soundtrack separately before preparing your post.

By Burning Bridges AI Video · October 20, 2026

What you can do here: AI Smalltown Boy provides reference material and preparation guidance. It does not generate this effect. The rendering steps below require a compatible external tool. Burning Bridges plans cover the separate party template.

Choose the version of the duo you want

Smalltown Boy can refer to several dance interpretations. In the duo covered here, the characters take turns at the front of the frame before sharing the dance. Watch the references on our project page and choose the sequence you actually want, rather than selecting an effect by song title alone.

Make a brief role map: character A leads first, character B starts behind, then they exchange attention. The final shared frame is a separate checkpoint. If the chosen template uses different positions or cuts, use its own role labels instead of assuming that every Smalltown Boy effect works alike.

Give both characters a readable source image

Use separate portraits with eyes, hairline and shoulders visible. Similar sharpness matters more than matching backgrounds. Extreme close-ups leave little information about the outfit, while a distant group shot makes it harder to isolate a face.

Our Smalltown Boy page previews two JPG, PNG or WebP images, up to 10 MB each. Both selectors work independently and the images remain on your device. That is useful for comparing a pairing, but it is not a test of two-character motion or a submission to a generator.

If you want a pet version, choose a provider that explicitly supports pets. Use a clear image with the whole head and distinctive markings visible. Being able to preview a cat photo in a browser does not establish that a human-duo model supports it.

For more framing checks, see the photo preparation guide; use this project’s stated file limits where they differ.

Confirm the role swap and audio behavior

Smalltownboy.app documents a first-lead and second-lead photo order and a silent output. Its page also describes an alternative combined-photo input. These are that provider's choices, not universal requirements; our local preview uses two separate images.

For a fixed template, the choreography may already be supplied. Do not add movement instructions that conflict with it. If you instead use a custom image-and-motion workflow, make sure it supports two distinct subjects and accepts a reference you can upload. A single-person motion mode may not preserve a duo.

Make and review your first video

  1. Record who leads first

    Choose character A and character B before opening the external tool. Keep that order in your filenames or notes so you can compare the finished close-ups with the intended cast.

  2. Check the selected template

    Watch for the first reaction, the swap, and the shared dance in its sample. If you prefer a different version, switch templates before uploading; a longer duration does not necessarily add a missing scene.

  3. Upload and verify the pairing

    Use the provider's documented fields and check both previews. For a combined-photo mode, confirm how the service assigns identities. Separate portraits are easier to compare when the tool accepts them.

  4. Read the output and cost

    Check the available frame shape, duration, sound and download conditions. Avoid assuming that a reference soundtrack will be included. Generate one draft after confirming the current quote.

  5. Review the swap and export

    Pause at the handover between characters. Confirm that facial features and clothing stay attached to the same identity. Listen to the saved file, add permitted sound if needed, and check that the final dance remains inside the crop.

Fix the most visible problem first

Only the first character looks right
Compare the sharpness and visible detail of the second source photo. Try a clearer image for that role while holding the other input constant.
The faces trade identities during the swap
Check the generator's role map and whether it supports two subjects. Inspect the transition rather than judging two isolated close-ups.
The reaction misses the beat
Check the chosen sound version and the edit's start point. Align a clear cue first, then replay the full sequence; one synchronized moment does not prove the rest stays in time.

Save the first draft and change one input at a time. The video troubleshooting guide explains how to separate input problems from a completed result that needs revision.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need one photo or two?

Our planning page uses two separate portraits. Some external tools accept one duo photo, but role assignment and supported inputs vary.

Can I use a cat and a person?

Only choose a generator that documents support for that pairing. A local preview does not verify pet generation or mixed-subject quality.

Is Smalltown Boy included in Burning Bridges plans?

No. The project page is for credited references and local photo preparation. Current plans support the separate Burning Bridges party template.
