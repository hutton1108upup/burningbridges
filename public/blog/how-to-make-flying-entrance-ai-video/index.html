How to Make a Flying Entrance AI Car Jump Video

To make a Flying Entrance AI video, prepare a photo of the person and a separate front view of the car. Choose a template that places the seated person in front of the airborne vehicle, then review the approach, arc and landing. The calm foreground and moving background must remain visually separate.

By Burning Bridges AI Video · October 20, 2026

Plan the seated foreground and car's path

The reference shows a person sitting calmly while a car rises behind them and lands in a dusty field. The contrast is the point of the scene. Keep the face readable while leaving enough space above and behind the chair for the vehicle's path.

Watch the reference and note the car's position before the jump, at the top of the arc and after landing. Decide whether you want a finger-snap ending or a dust reaction if the chosen template offers those options. These are fictional visual effects, not instructions for filming a real stunt.

A close crop of the person may look good in a thumbnail but hide the effect. Judge composition with the whole trajectory in mind, especially if you plan to turn a wider video into a vertical post.

Prepare a person photo and a separate car photo

Use a well-lit person photo with the face visible and the full body included where possible. Avoid a crowded background or objects covering the arms. The car photo should show the vehicle from the front with the entire body in frame.

Inspect the headlights, grille, mirrors and wheel placement in the source. These become useful checks when the car tilts during the jump. Strong reflections or an extreme camera angle can make those details harder to compare.

Our Flying Entrance workspace accepts JPG, PNG or WebP up to 10 MB per image. The person and car selectors are independent. They preview the files on your device without uploading them, submitting a render or spending credits.

For more framing checks, see the photo preparation guide; use this project’s stated file limits where they differ.

Use an effect that accepts both references

TalkPix documents Flying Entrance as a two-photo template with a person input, a car input and entrance options. Its page is useful for confirming the intended workflow. We have not purchased a render to verify its performance, and its settings are not Burning Bridges output settings.

A generic one-image animation mode may be suitable for an original car scene, but it does not automatically preserve a separate vehicle identity. If keeping your particular car matters, check for an explicit second reference input before uploading or paying.

For a custom prompt, describe the foreground pose and background path separately. Avoid asking the seated person to run, turn and gesture while the car jumps unless those extra actions are necessary. Each moving element creates another thing to inspect.

Make and review your first video

  1. Open the Flying Entrance effect

    Compare the provider's preview with the seated-person and airborne-car scene. Confirm it accepts both source photos; do not assume a general car filter offers the same composition.

  2. Assign the two photos correctly

    Place the person image in the subject field and the front-facing car image in the vehicle field. Check the previews for cropped heads, mirrors or bumper edges.

  3. Choose the entrance and output

    If endings are offered, select the one you want before rendering. Check the current duration, framing, audio and cost. Our reference length is not a promise of another tool's export settings.

  4. Review the full arc

    Follow the car from the background into the air and onto the ground. Pause at the highest point to inspect vehicle proportions and at the landing to inspect wheel contact and dust.

  5. Check the foreground and save

    The person's face, hands and chair should remain coherent while the background moves. Reopen the downloaded file, listen to its audio and confirm that the landing and ending remain in the final crop.

Fix the most visible problem first

The car changes shape in midair
Compare the grille, lights and wheels with the source image. Try a clearer front view or a simpler variation when available, without changing the person photo at the same time.
The vehicle appears in front of the chair
Check that the selected template matches the intended background jump. In a custom workflow, make the foreground and background placement explicit.
The dust hides the ending
Review whether the obscured frame is an intentional ending or a failed landing. If offered, a different entrance variation may leave the subject and car easier to see.

Save the first draft and change one input at a time. The video troubleshooting guide explains how to separate input problems from a completed result that needs revision.

Frequently asked questions

How many photos do I need?

The workflow covered here uses two: one person and one front-facing car photo. A single portrait cannot supply the appearance of your specific vehicle.

Will the car logo and body stay exact?

No exact preservation is guaranteed. Inspect headlights, grille, wheels and markings throughout the airborne motion and landing.

Does Burning Bridges sell the Flying Entrance effect?

No. Our page provides a reference and two local photo previews. The current credit plans cover the separate Burning Bridges party template.
