OUTSTANDING TINY DESK AI · HOW

How to Make an Outstanding Tiny Desk AI Video

Plan the cast before you create. This Outstanding Tiny Desk AI tutorial walks through the two photo layouts, the lead portrait, role choices and the checks to make before sharing your band remix.

By Burning Bridges AI Video · Updated October 26, 2026

1. Watch the scene before choosing the photos

Play the band reference through once, then pause where the camera moves closer to the lead and where an instrument becomes prominent. Notice which faces are large, which hands touch the keyboard and where the saxophone crosses the body.

Decide what your version should communicate. A group of friends, a work team or an adult family lineup is easier to read when the viewer can identify who is doing what. Keep the premise simple enough to understand before adding a caption.

2. Choose one group photo or individual portraits

Use One group photo if you have a clear image of the people together. Keep the cast to five or fewer adults and make sure no face is tiny or obscured. Plan the role order from left to right: lead, keyboard, sax and the two backing singers.

Use A photo each when separate portraits are clearer. Place the main person in Lead / frontman. Add the keyboard, sax and backing singers if you want to choose those appearances as well. You can leave the four optional photo positions empty.

3. Prepare the lead portrait carefully

The lead is the most useful photo to improve first. Choose a sharp face looking toward the camera, with visible hair and even light. A waist-up image also gives more information about the shoulders, sleeves and clothing than a tight headshot.

Use JPG, PNG or WebP files up to 10 MB. If your original phone file does not open, export a compatible copy and inspect it before selecting it. Enlarging a tiny face does not restore detail that was missing from the original.

4. Check the selections in the workspace

Open the maker and select Generate video in the Hero to reach the photo area. Pick the layout you want, then choose or replace the image in each relevant position. Confirm that the portrait belongs to the role named above it.

The active mode controls which photos are required. One group photo is required in group mode; the lead is required in individual mode. The workspace keeps you there if a required image is missing. Selecting a photo here displays it in your browser and does not itself submit a video job.

5. Review pricing and the creation details

Once the required photo is valid, the workspace’s Generate video button opens the pricing page. Review the current plans and the creation details before confirming a generation. Do not use another website’s credit price, output length or resolution as a specification for your chosen workflow.

Keep a copy of the source portraits and a simple role list. Those are useful if you later need to correct one person’s appearance. Change one input at a time so you can tell whether the next result addresses the problem you identified.

6. Inspect the band and the close-ups

  • Cast: check that nearby people remain distinct and the intended person appears in each part.
  • Lead: watch the face during the smile, head movement and laugh.
  • Instruments: inspect fingers, the keyboard edge and the saxophone shape.
  • Clothing: look for changes in collars, sleeves, logos and headwear.
  • Sound: listen to the exported edit, including its beginning and ending.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make an Outstanding Tiny Desk AI video?

Choose a group photo or role portraits, prepare the lead, review your selections in the maker and continue to pricing after the required image is valid. Check the creation details and review the complete result.

Do I have to add five separate photos?

No. You can use one group photo, or use individual-photo mode with one required lead photo and up to four optional band portraits.

Why does Generate video ask me to choose a photo?

The workspace requires a valid group image in group mode or a valid lead portrait in individual mode. Add the required image before continuing to pricing.

What should I fix if the instruments look wrong?

Inspect the hands and instrument boundaries through the moving clip. Use a clear portrait with visible shoulders and arms, and make a targeted change rather than changing the entire cast at once.
