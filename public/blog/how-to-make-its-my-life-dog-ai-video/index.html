How to Make an It's My Life Dog AI Video from a Pet Photo

Pick a clear pet portrait, plan the singing and sunglasses beats, and check the muzzle, paw and audio before sharing your car-seat video.

By Burning Bridges AI Video · November 6, 2026

In this guide
  • 1. Take the photo at pet height
  • 2. Preview the passenger
  • 3. Put the pause before the glasses
  • 4. Watch once muted, once with sound

1. Take the photo at pet height

Get down to eye level and frame one pet from the chest up. For a dark-coated dog, turn toward a window until you can see the eyes against the fur. For a long-eared dog, leave space on both sides. Skip sunglasses and toys that cover the mouth.

2. Preview the passenger

Open the pet photo area and choose a JPG, PNG or WebP up to 10 MB. Compare the preview with the original: can you still see both ears and the line of the muzzle? Replace the crop if needed. With a photo selected, Generate video opens Pricing. Review the scene and terms before confirming a task.

3. Put the pause before the glasses

If the creation tool accepts a scene description, try this starting point:

A highway rolls past in the upper panel. Below, the camera stays on my buckled-in pet. The pet sings, falls silent and looks straight ahead. One paw lifts dark glasses onto its face. Once the paw drops, the singing starts again.

Keep the coat markings and ear shape from your portrait. Change one instruction at a time if the glasses arrive too early.

4. Watch once muted, once with sound

On the muted pass, check that the road moves during the pause and that a single paw carries the glasses. Look for a second muzzle or frames where the pet changes breed. Then listen: the mouth should stop with the voice and restart after the reveal. Leave the split-screen border clear of captions when cropping for a phone.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a video of my dog singing?

You can prepare this scene from one still portrait. Use the examples to plan the timing rather than trying to make a pet perform.

What if the muzzle disappears behind the shades?

Use a less tightly cropped photo and keep the glasses over the eyes. Check the frame just before the paw leaves the face.
