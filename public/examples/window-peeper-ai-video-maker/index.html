Four beats that make the scene work

These stills come from the reference above. Follow the change in attention, rather than treating this as a dance or a face-filter effect.

01 · The window establishes the scene

The outside view frames a person in a warmly lit room.

02 · The camera gets closer

04 · The curtain closes

Plan a short window story

01

Choose your portrait

Use a sharp photo of yourself or someone who has agreed to appear. Avoid sunglasses, heavy shadows and a group of faces.

02

Keep the action simple

Frame the window, approach, notice the camera and close the curtain. Extra characters and simultaneous actions make the story harder to follow.

03

Review the whole result

Check the face, the window edges and the hand pulling the curtain. Judge the reaction at normal speed as well as paused.

Photo preparation

Give the face room to react

Leave space around the hair and shoulders. A tight crop can remove useful detail; a tiny face in a distant group shot is also a poor starting point.

Frequently asked questions

What is Window Peeper AI?

It is a photo-to-video scene built around an outside view of a lit window: the person notices the camera and closes the curtain. Caught at the Window describes the same scene idea here.

What photo should I use?

Choose one person facing forward with clear eyes, even light and visible shoulders. The photo area accepts JPG, PNG and WebP up to 10 MB.

Does choosing a photo start generation?

No. Selecting a photo displays a local preview. You can replace or remove it before continuing to the current plans.

Is Window Peeper AI free?

Watching the reference and preparing a photo here are free. Check the chosen generation and download terms before confirming a task; free photo preparation is not a promise of free rendering.

Should I film someone through a window?

Stage the scene with people who agree to appear. Use the window perspective as a fictional storytelling device, not as a reason to record unsuspecting people.
