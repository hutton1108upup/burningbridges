How Burning Bridges AI Video Works

Understand how appearance, motion and framing contribute to a party-video version. Choose a method before comparing its settings and costs.

A Portrait and a Prepared Performance

In a Drake Burning Bridges AI video workflow, a portrait supplies an appearance reference and a performance clip can supply movement and camera timing. A newly described party scene is another approach. These inputs guide a result; they do not guarantee that the lead, crowd and soundtrack will match an existing clip.

Use the Burning Bridges video tool to prepare your portrait, or follow the creation walkthrough for the full sequence. Use this overview to decide which reference you need before selecting settings.

Prepare One Clear Photo

The photo form accepts one image under 8MB. Choose one visible face in even lighting. Keep the head and shoulders visible; the current party template supplies the outfit. This is a single-photo workflow, not background-person casting.

Use the Template as a Reference

Study the gestures, camera sequence and soundtrack. A reference guides an edit; it does not guarantee unchanged guests, exact identity, flawless movement or perfect lip synchronization.

Check the Fixed Output Settings

The current template uses fixed 720P, 16:9 output at 100 credits per generation. Inspect framing and identity in the completed result; the output specification does not guarantee likeness or natural motion.

Compare Credit Options

Example playback and photo selection are free. Monthly plans start at $19 and one-time credit packs at $9.90. Check pricing for credits, validity and billing periods.

Review the Whole Result

Watch close-ups, hands, overlaps and the edges of the frame. Listen with sound on and check mouth movement. Keep results that suit your intended use.
